Market Tailwind is an automated research tool. Please don't rely on these recommendations to make investment decisions: you can lose money.

  • Not advice. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security. It is not personalised and knows nothing about your goals, finances, risk tolerance or taxes. Consider speaking to a licensed financial adviser.
  • Simple, fallible rules. Ideas come from price momentum, trend, volatility and a word count over news headlines. These rules can be wrong, and headline tone can be misread. Fundamentals shown are rules of thumb, not verdicts.
  • Imperfect data. Prices, news and company numbers come from Yahoo Finance's free feeds. They can be delayed, incomplete or wrong, and they are refreshed only a couple of times a day.
  • Backtests mislead. Past results are flattered by survivorship bias (today's index members and hand-picked ETFs and quantum stocks; companies that fell out are missing), simplified costs and luck. Past performance does not predict future returns.
  • Investing carries risk, including losing everything you put in. Short-term ideas (1 week to 1 month) are especially noisy.
  • Verify before you act. Check company filings, official sources and several independent opinions. Use this app only as a starting point for your own research.
  • No warranty. The app is provided as is, with no guarantee of accuracy, and its authors accept no liability for decisions made using it.

Analyst targets and ratings also lag the price, so after a sharp fall the "upside" can look large simply because the price dropped.

          Read before trusting these numbers
          • Survivorship bias: the stock list is today's S&P 500 members, so companies that were dropped or failed are missing. The Singapore stocks, quantum stocks and ETFs are hand-picked with hindsight, which flatters results even more. Past results look better than a real investor could have achieved. History also only goes back to 2020.
          • Costs are simplified: flat basis points per side, no taxes, no slippage.
          • Mixed currencies: US and SG stocks are ranked on local-currency returns.
          • Few years, few stocks: differences of a few percent are noise.
          • A good backtest does not predict future returns.

          Scoreboard: how the app's own picks did

          Every refresh, the app records its default top picks (Rankings for each horizon, Pullbacks, and the leader of each top sector) in a log on GitHub, before anyone knows how they turn out. This tab measures those exact lists afterwards: equal-weighted, bought at the next trading day's close, against SPY over the same days. Nothing is filled in after the fact, so unlike the Backtest this is genuine out-of-sample evidence. It is before costs and taxes, and it is a tiny sample at first: consecutive daily lists overlap, so look at the "independent windows" column, and don't read anything into it until there are dozens. It is not advice.

          How the score works: each stock is ranked against the others on 12-1 month momentum (35%), 6-1 month momentum (20%), trend vs. 50/200-day averages (20%), low volatility (15%) and closeness to the 1-year high (10%).

          Important: Market Tailwind is a research tool, not financial advice. The rankings, sector views and suggested stocks and ETFs are automated ideas to investigate, not instructions to buy or sell. Do your own research and don't rely on them. See "Read the full disclaimer" at the top of the page.

          Companies that report a loss: the ranking looks at price behaviour only, so a stock with negative ROE or net profit margin (about 4% of the list) can still rank high on momentum. These carry a ⚠ and a warning in their detail panel. In Controls, "Hide companies that report a loss" removes them from Rankings, Sectors and the chart list. It does not change the backtest, because it uses today's financials and applying them to the past would be look-ahead bias. A one-off write-down can also cause a reported loss, so check the reason.

          Lookup: the Lookup tab builds a full report for any stock or ETF Yahoo Finance knows, even one that is not in this app. A GitHub job fetches it (about 1 to 2 minutes, needs the same token as Refresh) and saves it on a public branch, so the symbols looked up are visible to anyone. The report lists points in its favour and of caution, from price trend, statements, analysts, earnings, insiders and news. It describes the company and is not advice.

          Scoreboard: the Scoreboard tab judges the app's own default picks using a log recorded on every refresh (committed to GitHub, so the history shows when each list was written). Results appear as the weeks pass and carry an "independent windows" count, because daily snapshots overlap. It is honest evidence but a small sample early on, and it is not advice.

          Event risk: a 📅 marker means the company is expected to report earnings within two weeks, when stocks often move sharply either way. Dates come from Yahoo and can shift; no marker means no date is known, not that nothing is coming.

          Risk check: the Holdings tab and the cash planner show how your positions have behaved together over up to three years: swings, worst falls, beta, correlation and a replay of the market's worst stretches. It describes the past and is not a forecast.

          Pullbacks: the Pullbacks tab lists stocks that fell by at least the amount you choose over a window you choose, ranked by how much supporting evidence they have: news tone, profitability, analyst targets and ratings, expected earnings (forward vs trailing P/E), sales growth, a reasonable valuation, and a six-month trend that is still up. You can narrow it to one sector with the optional Sector dropdown. It lists evidence for and against, and funds are left out because they have no company financials. It is a screen, not a forecast: it cannot be backtested (we only have today's analyst and financial data), and a falling stock is often falling for a good reason.

          ETFs and quantum stocks: besides the S&P 500 and 15 Singapore stocks, the ranking includes about 60 hand-picked ETFs (AI and tech themes, other themes, index and international, commodities, crypto and bonds, and the 11 sector ETFs) and 7 quantum-computing stocks plus the QTUM ETF. They are ranked with the same price-only score, so a fund and a stock are compared on momentum, trend and volatility alone. Funds have no company financials, so the fundamentals checks do not apply to them. Quantum stocks are small, very volatile and mostly unprofitable (see the loss warnings). Use the group buttons in Controls to include or exclude any of them.

          Holdings: the Holdings tab shows how the app's signals look for stocks you own (trend, news tone, ranking, fundamentals check, price moves) and lists what changed since the previous data update. Your holdings are kept only in this browser's storage. It is a monitor, not a source of buy or sell instructions.

          Sectors and News: the 11 US sectors are ranked by 70% price momentum (their SPDR sector ETF, using the horizon you picked) and 30% news tone (recency-weighted tone of the last 7 days of headlines for the ETF and every S&P 500 stock in that sector, compared across sectors). Stocks inside a sector use 75% price and 25% news. The top three stocks in each sector (out of all its S&P 500 members) and the sector ETF are the suggestions. The News tab lists the headlines behind each sector, ranked by impact: a headline's impact is its share of the sector's total tone signal (newer and stronger-toned headlines count more). Market-wide headlines at the top of the News tab are for context only and are not part of any score.

          Horizons: Long-term is the model above. 6 months, 3 months, 1 month, 2 weeks and 1 week replace both momentum terms (55% together) with the return over just that window; trend, volatility and closeness to the 1-year high stay the same. The shortest windows chase what moved most recently, which is noisy and often partly reverses, so treat them as watch-list ideas and compare them on the Backtest tab.

          Backtest: rebalanced on the last trading day of each month (Long-term, 1 month), every second week (2 weeks) or every week (1 week), matching the horizon you picked (6 months and 3 months rebalance monthly). The trade fills one trading day later, so the signal never sees prices it could not have acted on. Shorter horizons trade more often, so trading costs matter much more.
