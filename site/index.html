    Read before trusting these numbers
    • Survivorship bias: the watchlist is today's large, successful companies, so past results look better than a real investor could have achieved.
    • Costs are simplified: flat basis points per side, no taxes, no slippage.
    • Mixed currencies: US and SG stocks are ranked on local-currency returns.
    • Few years, few stocks: differences of a few percent are noise.
    • A good backtest does not predict future returns.

    How the score works: each stock is ranked against the others on 12-1 month momentum (35%), 6-1 month momentum (20%), trend vs. 50/200-day averages (20%), low volatility (15%) and closeness to the 1-year high (10%).

    Horizons: Long-term is the model above. 1 month, 2 weeks and 1 week replace both momentum terms (55% together) with the return over just that recent window; trend, volatility and closeness to the 1-year high stay the same. Short windows chase what moved most recently, which is noisy and often partly reverses, so treat them as watch-list ideas and compare them on the Backtest tab.

    Backtest: rebalanced on the last trading day of each month (Long-term, 1 month), every second week (2 weeks) or every week (1 week), matching the horizon you picked. The trade fills one trading day later, so the signal never sees prices it could not have acted on. Shorter horizons trade more often, so trading costs matter much more.
